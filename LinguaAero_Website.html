The Problem

A billion-dollar industry,
solved with hand gestures.

Language barriers at airline counters are daily, damaging, and completely unsolved. Every workaround in use today is inadequate.

✈️

9.5 Billion Passengers. One Language.

Global air traffic reached 9.5 billion in 2024, growing toward 19.5 billion by 2042. The majority of that growth comes from non-English-speaking Asia-Pacific, Middle Eastern, and African markets.

31.7% of global traffic from Asia-Pacific alone
🧳

Pakistan: 727,000 Workers Fly Gulf Routes Every Year

Over 727,000 Pakistani workers migrated to Gulf countries in 2024, the majority from the labour class with limited Arabic proficiency. They face language barriers at every counter from departure to arrival.

96% headed to Arabic-speaking Gulf countries
🌍

Pakistan Tourism Growing at 11% Per Year

Pakistan welcomed nearly 1 million international tourists in 2024, with the tourism market projected to reach USD 7.28 billion by 2030. These visitors speak dozens of languages and interact at every airport counter.

115% increase in tourist arrivals in 2023
⚠️

70% of Aviation Incidents Involve Communication Failure

Billings and Reynard (1981) found communication failures in 70% of aviation incidents. ICAO attributes over 1,100 deaths to language-related failures across aviation touchpoints between 1976 and 2000.

Language barriers are a safety issue, not just a service issue
How It Works

Five steps. Under one second.

LinguaAero runs on the existing counter computer. No new machines. No passenger setup. No training overhaul.

01

Passenger Speaks

Passenger speaks naturally in their language into the passenger-side microphone.

02

Speech to Text

Google Cloud STT converts audio to text in real time with automatic language detection.

03

Neural Translation

Google NMT translates to the staff's language in under 200 milliseconds.

04

Audio Output

Translated audio plays through the staff-side speaker in natural-sounding voice.

05

Staff Replies

Same pipeline in reverse. Staff responds, passenger hears it in their language.
